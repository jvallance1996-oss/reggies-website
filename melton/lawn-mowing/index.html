A tidy lawn for everyday use or a property handover.

A front lawn, a long backyard and an overgrown rental yard can need very different amounts of work. Tell Jack which areas you want included and whether the priority is ongoing upkeep, an inspection or bringing long grass back under control.

Front lawns, backyards and nature strips

Include every grassed area in your enquiry, especially nature strips and sections behind side gates. Mowing and edging around paths, driveways and garden borders help give the property a consistent finish. Point out obstacles or areas that need extra care.

Regular visits that suit the lawn

Discuss a repeat mowing arrangement based on grass growth and the season. An initial visit may involve more work if the lawn has become long or the edges have spread over paths. Jack can quote that first tidy-up separately from the ongoing maintenance requirements.

Rental inspections and one-off tidy-ups

For a rental inspection, move or sale preparation, send the required date with current photos. Show the longest grass as well as the overall yard. If rubbish or garden waste also needs attention, mention it in the same enquiry so the full job can be assessed.

Ask with the inspection date and current yard photos. Jack will confirm availability and the agreed work; mention edging, the nature strip and any other tidy-up tasks when requesting the quote.

Is the nature strip included?

It can be included. Show it in your photos and mention any additional street frontage so the quote covers the full area you want mown.

Does an overgrown lawn cost the same as regular mowing?

Long grass can require extra time and work. The initial quote takes the current condition into account, while repeat visits can be discussed once the lawn is manageable.

Can lawn mowing and rubbish removal be quoted together?

Yes. Include photos of the lawn and a separate view of the rubbish or green waste. Jack can assess both tasks and confirm what is included.
